Side A ✳ What we're looking for

We read a house differently.

A vintage shopper sees a dining set. We see the shelf behind it. These ten are what we catch in a listing's forty-eighth photograph.

Trk 01

Art

Signed prints, original works, gallery labels still taped to the back of the frame.

Trk 02

Books + paper

First editions, inscriptions, marginalia, and the library card left in the pocket.

Trk 03

Music

Soul, jazz, gospel and go-go. Liner notes, promo pressings, handwritten labels.

Trk 04

HBCU

Yearbooks, homecoming programs, pennants, alumni letters, band arrangements.

Trk 05

Divine Nine

Chapter records, paraphernalia, convention programs, jackets and pins.

Trk 06

Fashion + beauty

Furs and hats, salon equipment, counter compacts, brown liner and gloss.

Trk 07

Photography

Studio portraits, church directories, negatives, reunion and graduation albums.

Trk 08

Church + community

Anniversary books, programs, choir material, club minutes, funeral fans.

Trk 09

African material culture

Textiles, carving, brass, beadwork, and the paperwork of how it arrived.

Trk 10

Magazines + ephemera

Runs of Black periodicals, playbills, matchbooks, receipts, correspondence.

✳ Cultural Custody

Coming soon · free + public

A community-built record of Black material culture that survives in private hands.

Most of what survives was never accessioned. It stayed in a hall closet, a cedar chest, a garage. Cultural Custody will record what exists and where its meaning lives, without asking anyone to give anything up. The first experiment will likely focus on HBCU yearbooks and other paper records.

Preservation doesn't require possession.

✳ About

Safekeeping exists to recognize, research and surface Black material culture as it moves from private hands into the wider world.

We are not an estate-sale company and we do not sell what we find. We read listings the way a curator reads a room, and we tell our community what is there. History is not behind glass here. It is present tense, moving through hands, and worth knowing about while it moves.

Some things belong to a family. Their meaning can belong to a culture.
